# 🤝 Mentorship

Every project team has a mentor, and every member has someone to ask. Mentorship is how a few experienced people lift a whole community, and how newer members become the next mentors.

## Who can mentor

You do **not** need to be an industry expert. Good mentors:

- Have **done a little more** than the team: a semester ahead, an internship, a Kaggle medal, a project shipped
- **Listen first** and ask good questions
- Are **reliable** for a few hours across the semester
- Follow the [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md)

Mentors come from: senior students, alumni, lecturers who are interested in the track, and industry guests.

## How pairing works

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **Week 4** | Teams pitch ideas. Mentors listen and note who they would like to help. |
| **Week 5** | Maintainers match each approved team with a mentor, considering topic, availability and fit. |
| **Weeks 5 to 10** | A short **weekly check-in** (15 to 30 minutes) per team |
| **Week 8** | The mentor helps with the responsible AI review |
| **Week 10** | The mentor attends demo day and gives feedback |

A mentor can have **one or two teams**. Quality beats quantity.

## What mentors do

- ✅ Ask questions that help the team see the next step ("What would a baseline look like?")
- ✅ Review plans, notebooks and pull requests, kindly and specifically
- ✅ Share experience: mistakes you made, tools you like, how you would approach it
- ✅ Unblock: introduce people, point to the right doc
- ✅ Encourage, and celebrate progress

## What mentors do not do

- ❌ Write the team's code or take over the project
- ❌ Decide for them. Offer options and let the team choose.
- ❌ Grade or judge people
- ❌ Share private information about team members

## What mentees do

- Prepare a **short agenda** before each check-in: *progress, blockers, next step*
- **Own the work** and do what they agreed to do
- Say early when they are **stuck** or **short on time**
- Say thank you, and pass it on later

## Check-in template

Copy this into the project's issue or notes. Fifteen minutes is enough.

```markdown
## Check-in: <date>

**Since last time:** what we did
**Blockers:** what is in the way
**Questions for the mentor:**
**Next step (who, by when):**
**Responsible AI note:** anything new we should flag?
```

## Good practice

- **Keep it open.** Meet in group channels, in public or with at least two people. Don't arrange one-to-one meetings in private places.
- **Keep records short.** A note in the project issue is enough, so that others can follow and so a stand-in mentor can pick up.
- **Be on time and kind.** Cancel early if you must.
- **Declare conflicts.** If you are also a judge, a competitor or a close relative of a team member, tell a maintainer.
- **Raise concerns.** If something worries you about a team member's wellbeing or conduct, talk to the Track Lead. See the [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).

## Ending a pairing

Either side may end a pairing at any time, with no blame. Tell a maintainer and we will help find a replacement. At the end of the semester, mentor and mentee say what worked and what to change.

## Beyond project mentors

| Support | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Study buddies** | Pair up with someone on the same week's challenge. Check in mid-week. |
| **Office hours** | Weekly slot with maintainers (time pinned in the track channel). Bring your stuck notebook. |
| **Open mentors** | Anyone can ask a maintainer or mentor a question in the channel. |

## Become a mentor

Open an issue or tell the Track Lead. We will ask which topics you enjoy and how much time you can give, then introduce you to a team. Mentors are credited in the showcase and thanked at demo day.
