# 🏆 Competitions and Hackathons

Competing is one of the fastest ways to learn. You get a real problem, a deadline, honest feedback from a leaderboard and teammates who push you. We prepare together and enter as teams, and **taking part matters more than winning**.

## Where we compete

| Where | What it is | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| [Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions) | The best-known competition platform. Start with the *Getting Started* and *Playground* competitions. | Beginners, steady practice. Your first entry is a [Week 3](../weekly-sessions/week-03-evaluate-honestly/README.md) challenge. |
| [Zindi](https://zindi.africa) | Data science competitions focused on African problems | Local relevance and real datasets |
| **Google Kenya hackathon (13 Nov 2026)** | The Cloud Track plans to enter teams. AI/ML members are welcome to join. | Building fast with Google tools. **Check the official page for the current rules, eligibility, themes and deadlines.** |
| **DevFest** | The community-led developer events run by GDG chapters each year | Talks, workshops and networking. Watch the chapter announcements. |
| **Hack Night** (in-house) | A 3-hour build sprint after [Week 7](../weekly-sessions/week-07-rag-and-agents-study-jam/README.md) | A friendly first taste of building under time pressure |

## How we prepare

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **Before** | Finish the [Week 3 notebook](../weekly-sessions/week-03-evaluate-honestly/README.md). Join Kaggle. Read the rules of the competition. |
| **Team up** | Find 3 to 5 people with mixed skills (data, modelling, app, design, presenting) |
| **During** | Follow the baseline-first plan below |
| **After** | Share what you learned in the track channel and add a short write-up to the [showcase](../projects/showcase.md) |

## The baseline-first plan

The most common beginner mistake is spending days on a clever model before there is any working pipeline. Do this instead:

1. **Read the rules and the metric.** Know exactly what is being scored.
2. **Get a baseline submission in the first hour.** Even the simplest model gives you a score to beat and proves your pipeline works.
3. **Build a trustworthy validation set** that mimics the test set. If your local score does not track the leaderboard, fix that before anything else (see [Week 3](../weekly-sessions/week-03-evaluate-honestly/README.md)).
4. **Improve one thing at a time**, and keep notes: what you changed and what happened.
5. **Look at your mistakes.** Real examples of failures beat tuning blindly.
6. **Blend and simplify late.** Ensembling helps near the end. Leakage and overfitting to the leaderboard hurt.
7. **Write up** what you tried, including what failed.

## A 24-hour hackathon plan

| Hours | Do this |
|---|---|
| **0 to 1** | Understand the challenge. Pick **one** user and **one** problem. Decide roles. |
| **1 to 3** | Make the smallest thing that works end to end, even if it is ugly. A baseline, not a masterpiece. |
| **3 to 10** | Improve the part that matters most for the demo. Commit often. |
| **10 to 16** | Sleep in shifts if the format allows it. Fix the worst bugs. |
| **16 to 20** | Stop adding features. Evaluate honestly. Handle the failure cases. |
| **20 to 23** | Build the demo path, record a **backup video**, write the README. |
| **23 to 24** | Rehearse the pitch twice. Submit **before** the deadline. |

## Team checklist

- [ ] A team of the size the rules allow, with mixed skills
- [ ] Clear roles: who builds, who tests, who pitches
- [ ] A shared GitHub repo from the first hour, with a `.gitignore` and no secrets
- [ ] A one-paragraph problem statement
- [ ] A working demo **before** polish
- [ ] A baseline and an honest metric
- [ ] A short answer to "who could be harmed?" ([Responsible AI](../docs/responsible-ai.md))
- [ ] A 3-minute pitch practised at least twice
- [ ] Free-tier limits and API budgets checked in advance

## Pitch structure

1. **Problem:** who has it, and why it matters
2. **Solution:** what you built, in one sentence
3. **Demo:** show it working
4. **How it works and how you know:** the approach and the honest metric, in 30 seconds
5. **Impact, risks and next steps**

## Fair play

- Follow each competition's **official rules**. Check team sizes, external data, pretrained models and AI-tool policies.
- **Never share private competition data or code** outside your team, unless the rules allow it.
- Credit every dataset, model and library you used.
- Be a good competitor: help others, share lessons afterwards and congratulate the winners.

## After the competition

Share what you learned in the track channel. Add a short write-up to the [showcase](../projects/showcase.md) or your project README: what you built, what worked, what did not, and what you would do next time.
