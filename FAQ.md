# ❓ FAQ

## Do I need experience to join?

No machine learning experience is needed. Some programming helps, and Week 1 is a gentle Python-for-data session. If you have never coded, tell us. We will pair you with a mentor and share a pre-work list ([Stage 1](learning-path/01-foundations.md)).

## Do I need to be good at maths?

No. We build intuition first and use as little notation as we can. You will meet averages, a little probability and the idea of a slope. [Stage 1](learning-path/01-foundations.md) links to friendly, visual explanations.

## Is it free?

Yes. The sessions and resources are free, and everything runs on free tools (Colab, Kaggle, Google AI Studio). Free tiers have limits that change, so always check the current terms. We never ask you to pay for compute.

## My laptop is old or slow. Can I still take part?

Yes. Use **Google Colab** or **Kaggle Notebooks** in your browser. Tell the Track Lead if you struggle with internet or devices and we will help you find a setup. See the [compute guide](resources/compute-guide.md).

## Data is expensive for me. What can I do?

Datasets in this track are small on purpose. Download things once on campus Wi-Fi, use Kaggle and Colab where datasets are already hosted next to the compute, and ask us to share files in the room. The [compute guide](resources/compute-guide.md) has more tips.

## What if I miss a session?

Everything is in this repo. Open the week's folder, run the notebook, do the challenge and ask questions in the track channel.

## What language and tools do you use?

Python, with NumPy, pandas, matplotlib and scikit-learn for the basics, then Keras for deep learning and the Gemini API for generative AI. PyTorch is fully welcome in projects. See [decision 0004](docs/decisions/0004-framework-policy.md) for why.

## Why Google tools?

We are a Google Developer Group chapter, so we use Google's free tools where they fit. The ideas (data, models, evaluation, responsible AI) transfer to any framework and any cloud.

## Can I use ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot?

Yes, as a tutor and a coding helper. Two rules: you must **understand** what you submit, and you must **say so** in your pull request. Learning happens when you can explain your own code. See [Contributing](CONTRIBUTING.md#ai-assisted). For university coursework, follow your lecturers' rules.

## How do I get help?

Open a [question issue](https://github.com/GDG-TUM/AI-ML-Track-GDG-on-Campus-TUM/issues/new/choose), ask in the track channel, or message the Track Lead. See [SUPPORT.md](SUPPORT.md).

## How do I contribute?

Start with [adding your profile](members/README.md), then read the [contributing guide](CONTRIBUTING.md).

## Can I propose my own project?

Yes, that is the point. Read the [project guide](projects/README.md) and open a *Project proposal* issue. We review proposals within a week.

## Can I use data about real people?

Only with care. Never use personal data (photos of people, messages, grades, health or financial records) without clear consent, and never commit it to GitHub. Kenya's Data Protection Act, 2019 applies. Read the [Responsible AI guide](docs/responsible-ai.md) and talk to a maintainer first.

## I accidentally committed a secret. What now?

Tell a maintainer **immediately**. The secret must be **revoked first** and replaced, since deleting the commit is not enough. See the [security policy](SECURITY.md).

## Do I get a certificate?

Active members who complete the challenges and a project are recognised at demo day. For industry certifications, see [resources](resources/free-resources.md#certifications).

## How do I join a competition like Kaggle or a hackathon?

See [competitions](competitions/README.md). We prepare together and enter as teams.
